Today, more than 2,000 animals and plants remain listed as threatened under federal law. This visualisation tells their story — who they are, where they live, and how close to the edge they stand.

Section 1 — The Scale of the Problem

Who is at risk?

The scale of Australia's biodiversity crisis is staggering — but the numbers hide a disturbing pattern. Plants dominate the raw count, yet it is the smaller, less-visible animal groups — invertebrates, frogs and freshwater fish — that face the most extreme threat. Nearly half of all threatened invertebrates are already critically endangered, yet they attract a fraction of the conservation attention given to iconic species like the koala or quoll.

Critically Endangered — faces imminent extinction without intervention
Endangered — high risk of extinction in the wild
Vulnerable — at elevated risk if threats persist

Species vs Ecological Communities: How Threat Status Differs

Individual species and entire ecosystems are both at risk — but the pattern differs sharply. Almost no ecological communities are merely "vulnerable"; most are already critically endangered or endangered by the time they are listed.

💡 44% of listed ecological communities are critically endangered, versus 22% of species — ecosystems face a far higher bar before receiving protection, meaning they are severely compromised by the time they are listed.

The hidden crisis: invertebrates and freshwater life. While the koala and Tasmanian devil dominate conservation headlines, Australia's threatened invertebrates — over 100 species of beetles, spiders, butterflies and freshwater mussels — go largely unnoticed. Nearly half are already critically endangered. These species underpin soil health, pollination, and aquatic food webs. Their loss would be irreversible and catastrophic for entire ecosystems.
Section 2 — Where in Australia?

The geography of loss

Threatened species are not evenly distributed across Australia — the pattern reveals both genuine ecological pressure and the extraordinary biodiversity of certain regions. New South Wales carries the heaviest burden by far. But Western Australia's count reveals a different story: vast endemic flora found nowhere else on Earth, pushed toward extinction by land clearing and invasive species.

NSW: the epicentre of Australia's extinction crisis. With 721 threatened species and 60 threatened ecological communities, New South Wales faces pressure on every front — urban sprawl pushing into the Blue Mountains, land clearing in the wheat belt, invasive species in river systems, and climate-driven fire and drought reshaping alpine and coastal habitats. The 2019–20 Black Summer fires destroyed habitat for over 100 threatened species in NSW alone.
Section 3 — Ecosystem Collapse

When entire ecosystems are listed as threatened

The most alarming indicator in Australia's biodiversity crisis is not individual species — it is whole ecosystems. When an ecological community earns a threatened listing under the EPBC Act, it means not one species but an entire web of life — its plants, animals, fungi, soils and hydrology — has declined to the point of requiring federal protection. Australia currently lists 111 such communities, and nearly half are already critically endangered.

Most Widespread Threatened Communities — How Many States Are Affected?

Some of Australia's most iconic ecosystems span multiple states — and all of them are in decline. Communities listed in 4 or more states have collapsed so broadly that recovery requires coordinated federal action across jurisdictions. Colour indicates threat level.

⚠ River Red Gum Grassy Woodlands and Murray-Darling Depression Eucalypt Woodlands are each listed across 4 states — meaning that two of Australia's most iconic inland ecosystems have collapsed at a continental scale.

Status Breakdown: 111 Threatened Ecological Communities

Of Australia's 111 listed ecological communities, 49 are critically endangered, 60 are endangered, and only 2 are merely vulnerable. This skewed distribution reveals a systemic failure — communities are rarely protected until they are already severely compromised.

💡 Only 2 of 111 listed communities are Vulnerable. The bar for listing is so high that by the time an ecosystem receives protection, it is already in crisis.

State Pressure: Species vs Communities — How Does NSW Compare?

Section 4 — Animal Groups in Detail

The animals that need help most urgently

Within Australia's threatened fauna, some groups are in far greater danger than their numbers suggest. While birds (164 species) are the most numerous threatened vertebrate group, frogs and freshwater fish face the most acute crisis proportionally. More than a third of all threatened fish and frogs are already critically endangered — rates that dwarf those seen in mammals or birds. Australia's extinction debt is not yet paid. Since 1788, Australia has lost 34 mammal species — more than any other country on Earth. Feral cats alone kill an estimated 1.5 billion native animals per year. Climate change is accelerating habitat loss, with alpine species like the mountain pygmy possum and the corroboree frog already running out of suitable territory. The species listed today are tomorrow's extinctions unless the rate of habitat loss, invasive species impact, and climate disruption is urgently reversed.
