Australia's Biodiversity Emergency

The Threatened Species
Crisis

Australia is one of the world's most biodiverse nations — yet it also holds the unenviable record as a global leader in species extinction. More than 2,000 animal and plant species are currently listed as threatened under Australian federal law. This visualisation explores who they are, where they live, and how urgent the situation has become.

Section 1 — The Scale of the Problem

Who is at risk?

Australia's threatened species list spans every kingdom of life. Plants make up the largest share by far — nearly 1,500 plant species are at risk. But proportionally, some animal groups face an even more dire outlook, with frogs and invertebrates having the highest share of critically endangered listings.

Section 2 — Where in Australia?

The geography of loss

Threatened species are not evenly distributed across Australia. NSW carries the heaviest burden with over 780 threatened species and communities — nearly double the next highest states. Western Australia and Queensland are also major hotspots, particularly for plant species.

Threatened Ecological Communities by State

Beyond species — entire ecosystems are also on the threatened list. NSW has the most.

NSW leads Australia in biodiversity loss. With 721 threatened species and 60 threatened ecological communities, New South Wales is by far the hardest-hit state. This reflects both genuine ecological pressure — from land clearing, urban sprawl and invasive species — and the state's high biodiversity relative to other jurisdictions.
Section 3 — Ecosystem Collapse

Threatened ecological communities

Beyond individual species, entire ecological communities — forests, wetlands, grasslands and coastal systems — are also protected under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999. Australia currently lists 111 threatened ecological communities. Some span multiple states, making coordinated conservation across jurisdictions essential.

Section 4 — Animal Groups in Detail

A crisis across the animal kingdom

While plants account for the sheer number of threatened listings, Australia's animals face a particularly alarming situation. Frogs, invertebrates and fish have extremely high proportions of critically endangered species — groups that receive far less public attention than iconic mammals.

Birds

164 species threatened — the most of any vertebrate animal group in Australia.

Mammals
