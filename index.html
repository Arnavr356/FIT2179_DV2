Australia is one of the world's most biodiverse nations — yet it holds the unenviable record as a global leader in species extinction. More than 2,000 animal and plant species are currently listed as threatened under Australian federal law. This visualisation explores who they are, where they live, and how urgent the situation has become.

Section 1 — The Scale of the Problem

Who is at risk?

Australia's threatened species list spans every kingdom of life. Plants make up the largest share by far — nearly 1,500 plant species are at risk. But proportionally, some animal groups face an even more dire outlook, with invertebrates, frogs and fish having the highest shares of critically endangered listings — groups that receive far less public attention than iconic mammals.

Threat Status: Species vs Ecological Communities

Individual species and whole ecosystems are both under threat. Communities face a stark lack of vulnerable listings; most that are listed are already critically endangered.

💡 Nearly 44% of listed ecological communities are critically endangered, vs only 22% of species.

The hidden crisis among invertebrates. While mammals like the koala and quoll receive significant media attention, Australia's threatened invertebrates — spiders, beetles, butterflies and freshwater mussels — number over 100 species, with 43% already critically endangered. These animals are vital to soil health, pollination and food webs, yet receive a fraction of the conservation funding directed at vertebrates.
Section 2 — Where in Australia?

The geography of loss

Threatened species are not evenly distributed across Australia. New South Wales carries the heaviest burden — with over 780 threatened species and communities — nearly double the count in Western Australia or Queensland. However, WA and QLD are major hotspots for plant species, reflecting their extraordinary floral biodiversity.

Section 3 — Ecosystem Collapse

Threatened ecological communities

Beyond individual species, entire ecological communities — forests, wetlands, grasslands and coastal systems — are also protected under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (EPBC Act). Australia currently lists 111 threatened ecological communities. Some span multiple states, making coordinated conservation across jurisdictions essential.

Most Widespread Threatened Communities (Number of States Listed In)

These ecological communities are listed as threatened across multiple states simultaneously — an indication of how broadly some Australian ecosystems have declined. Communities spanning 4 states face the greatest multi-jurisdictional conservation challenge.

Ecological Community Status Breakdown

Section 4 — Animal Groups in Detail

A crisis across the animal kingdom

Australia's animals face a particularly alarming situation. While birds are the most numerous threatened vertebrate group, frogs and fish have the highest proportions of critically endangered species. ⚠ 28 of 79 threatened fish species (35%) are critically endangered — a higher rate than mammals or birds.

Australia's extinction record is a global outlier. Since European colonisation in 1788, Australia has lost more mammal species than any other country on Earth — 34 native mammal species are now extinct. The introduction of feral cats, foxes, and rabbits, combined with land clearing, has pushed dozens more to the brink. Today, over 100 mammal species remain listed as threatened, and without continued action, more extinctions are inevitable.
